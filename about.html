About me

I’m Miebaka, a brand and visual designer in Lagos. I ask a lot of questions, mostly the kind that stop a team from decorating the wrong idea.

I started during NYSC in 2017, then learned through years of making, presenting, printing, revising and getting better. Today, I design at Rise across six marketing teams.

I like the whole job: finding the idea, making the case for it, giving it form, and staying long enough to see whether it survives the campaign, the handoff and the printer.

What I do

I design brand systems, campaigns and graphic worlds that can survive beyond the presentation. Strategy gives the work a reason; art direction gives it a recognisable life; production makes sure other people can actually use it.

The strongest projects usually begin with one useful decision. A pause. Access. Appetite. Once that decision is clear, typography, imagery, motion and applications can belong to the same idea.

How I work

  1. 01

    Ask the annoying question early.

    If nobody can explain the idea plainly, a better typeface will not rescue it.

  2. 02

    Make one decision carry the weight.

    A useful filter makes the next hundred choices less arbitrary.

  3. 03

    Stay for the unglamorous part.

    The handoff, file name, printer and next person using the system all count.

Where I’m useful

  1. Find the governing decision

    Turn an unclear brief into one decision that can guide the work.

    Typical outputs
    Positioning, messaging principles and a focused creative brief.
    Good fit
    Teams that need alignment before visual design begins.

  2. Build the identity system

    Translate that decision into a recognisable, usable visual language.

    Typical outputs
    Identity direction, visual systems and practical guidelines.
    Good fit
    Teams building a new identity or making an existing one coherent.

  3. Make it survive production

    Test the system in the formats, constraints and handoffs it must endure.

    Typical outputs
    Campaign assets, editorial layouts, packaging and production-ready artwork.
    Good fit
    Teams taking designed work from presentation to reliable production.

  4. Direct the rollout

    Keep many assets, makers and channels inside one coherent world.

    Typical outputs
    Creative direction, image language and production guidance.
    Good fit
    Teams coordinating a campaign, launch or growing brand system.

  5. Hand over a usable system

    Leave the team with clear tools, files and rules they can keep using.

    Typical outputs
    Practical guidelines, production files and implementation support.
    Good fit
    Teams that need the work to remain coherent after the presentation.

If that’s the kind of thinking your team needs, work with me or view the work.
